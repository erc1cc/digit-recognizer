The network

Two convolutional blocks — 32 channels then 64, each a pair of 3×3 convolutions with batch normalization, ReLU, max pooling and dropout — feeding a 128-unit hidden layer and a ten-way output. It was trained from scratch on MNIST for fourteen epochs with AdamW and a one-cycle learning rate schedule. That took two and a half minutes on a laptop and reached 99.62% on the 10,000-image test set: 38 mistakes, most often a 9 read as a 4.

Why it survives real handwriting

MNIST digits are clean, upright and perfectly centered. Digits drawn by an actual person are none of those things, so training applied random rotation, translation, scaling, shear and small erasures to every image. That costs a little accuracy on the benchmark and buys a lot on the pad above.

The preprocessing nobody mentions

MNIST images were not simply resized to 28×28. Each digit was cropped to its bounding box, scaled so its longest side is 20 pixels, then placed in a 28×28 field centered by its center of mass — not its bounding box. Feed a naively resized drawing to a model trained on that distribution and predictions fall apart in a way that looks like a broken model. The small panel under the pad shows the result of reproducing that exact normalization, which is why it is on screen rather than hidden.

Running in a browser

Each batch-norm layer was folded into the weights of the convolution feeding it — algebraically identical at inference time, and it leaves only convolution, ReLU, max pooling and one matrix multiply to reimplement in JavaScript. The weights travel as a base64 float32 block inside this page. The JavaScript output matches PyTorch to within 6e-7, which is float32 rounding rather than a difference in the math.

What it cannot do

One digit at a time. Reading a multi-digit number needs a segmentation step first. It also knows only the ten digits, so a letter produces a confident and meaningless answer — the softmax has to choose something. And it learned from American handwriting collected in the 1990s, so crossed sevens and European-style ones are underrepresented and read less reliably.
